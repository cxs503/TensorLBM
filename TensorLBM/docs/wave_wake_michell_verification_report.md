## 1. Case Introduction (Physical Background / Geometry / Parameters)

### 1.1 Physical Problem

Wave-making resistance is the component of ship resistance arising from the steady free-surface wave system generated by the hull. Unlike viscous resistance (which depends on Reynolds number and boundary layer state), wave resistance is governed by **Froude similarity** and is set by the interference of transverse and divergent waves in the Kelvin wake pattern.

The closed-form theoretical anchor is **Michell's thin-ship integral** (Michell 1898), which expresses wave resistance as a spectral integral over the hull's longitudinal slope distribution:

$$R_W = \frac{4\rho g^2}{\pi U^2} \int_0^{\pi/2} \sec^3\theta \left[P^2(\theta) + Q^2(\theta)\right] d\theta$$

where $P+iQ = \iint f_x e^{kz} e^{ikx\cos\theta} dx dz$, $k = k_0\sec^2\theta$, $k_0 = g/U^2$.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Michell Thin-Ship Theory Implementation

The implementation is in `src/tensorlbm/wave_wake_michell.py` with two independent computational routes:

1. **Sampled quadrature** (`michell_resistance`): Cell-centered sampling of $f_x$ on an $(n_x \times n_z)$ grid, with blocked matrix-vector evaluation of the $\theta$ integral. Uses $u=\tan\theta$ substitution for smooth integrand.

2. **Closed-form spectrum** (`michell_resistance_analytic`): For parabolic hulls only, the double integral collapses analytically to:
   $$P+iQ = \frac{B}{2}\left(-\frac{8}{L^2}\right) I_z(\kappa) X(m)$$
   where $I_z$ is the depth integral and $X$ is the axial Fourier transform. Only the $\theta$ quadrature remains.

The agreement between these two routes (sharing no discretization) certifies the source strength $f_x$, depth weighting $e^{\kappa z}$, and axial phase together.

### 2.2 Wake Geometry

- **Kelvin caustic angle**: Computed by bisection on the stationary-phase condition, verified against the analytic result $\alpha = \arcsin(1/3) \approx 19.47°$.
- **Wake field synthesis**: Stationary-phase evaluation of the free-surface elevation $\zeta(r,\alpha)$ on a polar grid.
- **Hump/hollow extraction**: Peak-finding on the $C_W$(Fn) curve with prominence-based detection.

### 2.3 Catamaran Interference

The thin-ship interference factor for two identical hulls at separation $S$ is:
$$\text{Factor} = \frac{C_W^{\text{catamaran}}}{C_W^{\text{monohull}}} = \frac{\int \sec^3\theta |P+iQ|^2 |1 + e^{ikS\cos\theta}|^2 d\theta}{\int \sec^3\theta |P+iQ|^2 d\theta}$$

### 2.4 LBM Free-Surface (2D Dispersion Audit)

The existing 2D LBM free-surface runs (`benchmarks/wave_wake_michell/lbm_runs/`) provide mass conservation and dispersion data. The 3D ship-scale run is not affordable (estimated >50 hours at 4 threads for λ/Δx≥20).

## 7. Limitations and Notes

1. **Thin-ship approximation**: Michell theory is first-order in beam/length. The admission window (Cb≤0.60, L/B≥5) is enforced. Series 60 (Cb=0.60) is at the edge.

2. **3D LBM not affordable**: The full 3D free-surface LBM simulation at ship scale requires a grid of ~256×128×64 cells and ~20,000 time steps for λ/Δx≥20. Estimated wall time >50 hours at 4 threads. This is a resource limitation, not a methodological gap.

3. **Closed-form spectrum limited to parabolic hulls**: The analytic spectrum integration is only available for the parabolic vertical profile. For Wigley III (power profile), the fine-grid sampled quadrature serves as the reference.

4. **No experimental wave height comparison**: The Series 60 wave height vs Noblesse et al. (1986) comparison requires 3D LBM free-surface, which is blocked. The Michell reference is available but cannot be compared to experiment without the LBM leg.

5. **Froude scaling verified**: The $C_W$(Fn) invariance under hull scale change is verified to machine precision (1.77×10⁻¹³ relative difference), confirming the Froude similarity law.
