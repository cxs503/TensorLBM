# Pancake ice: formation dynamics and ship resistance in a pancake field

Campaign node **IN69-N07** (TensorLBM). Code: `src/tensorlbm/pancake_ice.py`;
driver: `tools/run_pancake_resistance.py`; tests: `tests/test_pancake_ice.py`
(35 cases); artifact: `artifacts/pancake_ice/pancake_resistance.json`; all
numbers in `docs/pancake_ice_results.md` (auto-generated by
`--assemble`, nothing hand-transcribed).

## 1. Physical setting

In a freezing, wave-agitated sea the first ice is *frazil*: millimetre disc
crystals suspended in supercooled water. Under waves they collide and flocculate
into grease ice, and once the floes can no longer be dispersed by the wave
orbital motion they consolidate into **pancake ice** — circular discs
`D = 0.3-3 m`, `h = 0.05-0.3 m`, with a raised consolidated rim
(`h_rim = 0.02-0.05 m`, Lange et al. 1989; Martin & Kauffman 1981). A pancake
field is *not* a continuous ice sheet: a ship does not crush it, it slides
through and pushes it aside, so neither level-ice nor brash-ice resistance
formulas apply directly.

## 2. Model

### 2.1 Frazil → pancake criterion (collision frequency × coalescence probability)

Collision *kernel* of suspended crystals (three additive mechanisms, all
standard):

```
beta_wave      = (4/3) S (r_i+r_j)^3            Smoluchowski orthokinetic, S = a*omega*k*exp(kz)
beta_turbulent = 1.294 (eps/nu)^0.5 (r_i+r_j)^3 Saffman & Turner (1956)
beta_settling  = (pi/4) (r_i+r_j)^2 |w_i-w_j|   w = (rho_w-rho_i) g d^2/(18 mu)
```

collision frequency per floe `f = beta n`, sticking window
`p_stick = exp[-(u_rel/u_c)^2]` (`u_c = 0.02 m/s` for crystal–crystal,
`1 m/s` for slush onto a rim).

The step that turns a floe into a *pancake* is **retention against the wave
orbital motion**. The orbital semi-excursion `A_orb = a exp(kz)` is the stroke
of the water parcel that carries the frazil past the floe: below it the slush is
flushed away, above it the floe keeps it and grows an emergent rim by wave
pumping. Hence the consolidation probability

```
p(D) = 1 / (1 + (A_orb/D)^s),  s = 30   (10–90 % transition over D/A_orb = 0.93–1.08)
```

For a storm wave of steepness `H/lambda = 0.1`, `A_orb = H/2 = lambda/20` —
exactly Lange et al.'s observed pancake-size rule. `p(lambda/20) = 0.5` by
construction; the transition cannot be a step function (a floe does not switch
state at an exact diameter), so `s` parameterises a narrow distribution of
consolidation times.

A floe is also rejected when it does not float (`draft < h`, Archimedes with the
rim mass) or when the wave bending stress
`sigma = 0.75 rho_i (1+km) a omega^2 D^2/h` exceeds the flexural strength.
Setting `sigma = 0.3 MPa`, `h/D = 0.1` gives a survivable diameter of
`~lambda/10`, the observed upper bound of pancake size.

### 2.2 Geometry and buoyancy

Disc + rim ridge, `D/h in [5, 20]`, rim volume `pi D w_rim h_rim`, draft and
freeboard from Archimedes. The face-on added mass of a thin disc (Lamb §108,
`m_am = rho_w D^3/3`) is **~4.7× the pancake's own mass** at `D/h = 10`: this,
not friction, is why laterally displacing pancakes is expensive.

### 2.3 Resistance: slip + bulldozing

Two dissipation channels, weighted by a jamming split
`f_slip = 1/(1+(C/C_jam)^p)`, `C_jam = 0.65`, `p = 3` (sideways displacement
needs free space `∝ 1-C`; above `C_jam` the rafter field forms a contact network
and the whole raft must be pushed):

```
slip        R = k_s C B_eff V^2 [ rho_i h_eff + (4/3pi) rho_w D ]      (momentum flux + added mass)
                + 0.5 k_s^2 C B_eff rho_w C_d D V^2                    (face-on drag, C_d from LBM)
bulldozing  R = 0.5 rho_w C_d,raft A_front V^2 + C rho_i h_eff B_eff g (k_z h_pile)   (form + lift)
```

with rafting/thickening `h_eff = h [1 + k_raft (C/C_jam)^2]`. Both channels are
proportional to `C`, so `R(C=0) = 0` **identically** — the model is an *added*
ice resistance that superposes on an open-water curve.

All coefficients are declared a priori in `pancake_ice.DEFAULT_PARAMS`
(`k_slip = 0.35`, `C_jam = 0.65`, `p = 3`, `k_raft = 0.5`, pile/rise = 0.5,
`B_eff = 20 m`) and are not tuned to the acceptance numbers; §6 of
`docs/pancake_ice_results.md` is the ±50 % sensitivity study.

## 3. Numerical input: LBM drag table

`tools/run_pancake_resistance.py --lbm` runs the package's D2Q9 bounce-back
kernel (momentum-exchange force monitor) on a 2-D vertical section of a pancake
— a thick flat plate of aspect ratio `D/h` with the rim ridge, i.e. the geometry
the pancake presents when deflected face-on. 180×140 lattice, `u_in = 0.06`,
10 000–11 000 steps, drag averaged over the last 50 %.

The monitor is validated on the circular cylinder and corrected for channel
blockage with the continuity factor `(1-B)^2` (`B` = reference length / channel
height):

| case | `C_d` raw | `C_d` corrected | reference | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cylinder Re=100 | 2.511 | **1.386** | 1.34 (Braza et al. 1986) | **+3.4 %** |
| cylinder Re=40 | 3.645 | 2.012 | ~1.55 | +30 % (see limitations) |

Pancake sections (corrected): `C_d = 1.689 (D/h=5)`, `1.680 (D/h=10)`,
`1.637 (D/h=20)` at Re=400, and `2.265` at Re=50 — decreasing with Reynolds
number towards a plateau of **1.67**, inside the blunt-plate band 1.6–2.4. The
field-scale Reynolds number is `O(10^6)`, far above what a 2-D LBM grid can
resolve, so the plateau value is used as `C_d` in the resistance model (stated
explicitly rather than silently extrapolated).

## 4. Results

See `docs/pancake_ice_results.md` (auto-generated); headline numbers:

* **(a) coalescence** — `min p(D > lambda/20) = 0.959`, `max p(D < lambda/20) = 0.054`,
  `p(lambda/20) = 0.500` → criterion met.
* **(b) geometry** — 25/25 design geometries inside `D/h in [5,20]`,
  `h in [0.05,0.3] m`, all floating with positive freeboard.
* **formation time** — consolidation of a floe from the wave-selected seed
  `A_orb` to the breakup-limited diameter takes 0.07–0.16 h, while the
  thermodynamic production of a 10 cm slush layer at 200 W/m² takes **42.5 h**;
  the pancake-field formation time is therefore production-limited and equals
  **42.6 h** for `lambda = 10-30 m`, consistent with the observed one-to-two-day
  formation of pancake fields.
* **(c) scaling** — global log-log fit `R = a C^alpha V^beta` over the
  1200-point design: `alpha = 1.026`, `beta = 1.898`, `R² = 0.704`; per-diameter
  fits give `alpha = 0.99–1.11`, `beta = 1.84–1.94` with `R² = 0.97–0.997`.
  `alpha` sits at the lower edge of the accepted `[1,2]` band because the slip
  channel is linear in `C` by construction (it is a momentum flux); the only
  superlinearity is the rafting thickness. Enabling the rafting-cluster size
  correction `k_cluster = 1.0` raises `alpha` to 1.216 (§6).
* **(d) mechanism split** — `slip = 0.998` at `C = 0.1`, `bulldozing = 0.596` at
  `C = 0.8`; the crossover is at `C ≈ 0.7`.
* **(e) negative control** — `R(C=0) = 0.000e+00 N` exactly, over 30 (D, V) pairs.
* **(f) tests** — 35/35 pytest cases pass.

## 6. Limitations and honest caveats

1. **Sub-threshold growth is not resolved.** Orthokinetic coagulation of a
   monodisperse suspension grows exponentially with an e-folding time `~3/S`
   (seconds), which is a gelation artefact unless floc breakup is modelled.
   The pancake diameter is therefore taken as *wave-selected* (`A_orb`) and the
   growth ODE describes only the consolidation stage. The mm → dm grease-ice
   stage is declared **WITHHELD**.
2. **LBM is 2-D and confined.** Blockage is corrected with `(1-B)^2`, which
   brings the Re=100 cylinder to +3.4 % of the literature value, but the Re=40
   case remains +30 %: at low Reynolds number the steady state needs
   `~L^2/nu ≈ 10^6` steps, far beyond the compute budget, so the measured drag
   is a developing-flow value. The Re=40 case is reported, not used.
   Drag histories still drift by 10–25 % between the first and second half of the
   averaging window (`cd_first_half_mean` / `cd_second_half_mean` in the
   artifact), so the LBM coefficients carry ±15 % uncertainty.
3. **No closed-form field-resistance benchmark exists** for pancake ice, so the
   absolute resistance values are order-of-magnitude. The criteria are
   *structural* (scaling exponents, mechanism split, negative control), not
   comparisons against measured ship resistance.
4. **`alpha` is at the edge of its band** (1.026 vs `[1,2]`). This is a model
   property (linear momentum-flux slip channel), not a numerical fit; the
   sensitivity table shows the band `alpha = 0.87–1.51` under ±50 % parameter
   variation, i.e. `alpha < 1` for `k_slip` and `cd_raft` at +50 %.
5. **The jamming parameters are phenomenological.** `C_jam = 0.65`, `p = 3`,
   `k_raft = 0.5` are declared, not calibrated; the high-concentration split is
   the least robust result (it drops below 0.5 if `k_slip` is 1.5× or
   `C_jam` 1.5×, see §6 of the results file).
6. **Error against benchmark: not < 3 %.** The only true benchmark in this node
   is the cylinder drag coefficient (3.4 % error); every other acceptance
   number is a threshold check on the model itself, and no experimental pancake
   resistance target is used (none available). This is stated rather than
   glossed: the node delivers a *mechanistically closed, self-consistent and
   falsifiable* model, not a validated quantitative resistance prediction.
