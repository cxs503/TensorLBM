## 1. Case introduction

Frazil ice — millimetre-scale disc crystals nucleated in supercooled, turbulent water — forms a **suspension** whose rheology is the missing link between the thermodynamic state (ice volume fraction) and the hydrodynamic response used by resistance, propulsion and thrust-loss models. The engineering question is: *given the ice volume fraction, what is the effective viscosity the flow solver must use, and how does it depend on shear rate?*

This node implements that link as an explicit closure chain and validates it against analytic and literature references. It also carries a **particle-resolved face**: a frozen random suspension voxelised into a D3Q19 moving-wall Couette cell, from which the relative viscosity is *measured* rather than restated. The first attempt at that face was audited L1 (`docs/AUDIT_m14882_H2_FRAZIL_RHEOLOGY.md`: a body-force channel mislabelled Couette, a 2.37x pure-fluid calibration defect, `eta_r` taken as a ratio of *nominal* rates, and the closures compared at the declared instead of the achieved concentration). The reworked face is reported separately in §3.7, with its own pre-declared criteria and its own honest verdict.

The chain (one physical source of truth, the W9-1215 root `tensorlbm.frazil_rheology`):

```
phi -- Krieger-Dougherty --> mu_r(phi) = (1 - phi/phi_max)^(-[eta] phi_max)
    -- consistency index ---> K(phi)   = mu_water * mu_r(phi)          [Pa s^n]
    -- flow index ----------> n(phi)   = 1 - (1 - n_packed)(phi/phi_max)^1.5
    -- power law -----------> mu_app   = K(phi) * gdot^(n(phi)-1)      [Pa s]
```

## 2. What was done

1. **Closure module** `src/tensorlbm/frazil_suspension_rheology.py`: public entry `effective_viscosity(phi, gdot)`; the Krieger-Dougherty form is *delegated* to the W9-1215 root (no second copy of the physics); independent reference curves (Einstein 1906, Batchelor-Green 1972, Thomas 1965) live here for validation only.
2. **Four machine-checked criteria**, thresholds predeclared in the module:
   `c1` dilute limit vs Einstein (10 %), `c2` mid concentration vs Krieger-Dougherty plus the independent Thomas (1965) correlation (10 %), `c3` the W9-1215 root's own criteria including its 2 % channel criterion, `c4` the shear-rate scan must be a power law (fitted log-log slope = n(phi) − 1).
3. **Solver hand-off** `tensorlbm.frazil_lbm.run_frazil_poiseuille`: the physical `(K, n)` pair is passed unscaled into the D2Q9 power-law solver (non-equilibrium-stress closure for the local relaxation time, Guo forcing); the LBM velocity profile is compared with the exact Ostwald-de Waele Poiseuille similarity solution *for the same `(K, n, a)`*. Where the physical Krieger-Dougherty consistency would push τ far above 1 the pair is lattice-rescaled by λ (`K→λK`, `a→λa`), under which the velocity field is invariant; λ is recorded per case.
4. **Entry point** `scripts/run_h2_frazil_rheology.py` writes `campaign.json`, `benchmark.md`, `repro.json` and three figures; `scripts/make_h2_frazil_doc.py` renders this document from `campaign.json` (and from `couette_measurement.json` for §3.7, when that artifact is present).
5. **Particle-resolved face** `scripts/run_h2_frazil_couette.py` on the mature shear face `tensorlbm.frazil_powerlaw_mature`: moving walls exchange momentum with the fluid, the stress is read twice (plane momentum flux in the particle-free wall band, and the per-wall momentum-exchange force), `eta_r` is a ratio of *measured* viscosities, and every closure is evaluated at the *measured* lattice volume fraction `phi_lattice` = union solid nodes / physical cell volume.

## 5. Notes, pitfalls, limitations

1. **The particle-resolved face was reworked, not merely re-run.** The audited attempt (`docs/AUDIT_m14882_H2_FRAZIL_RHEOLOGY.md`) measured η_r = 2.6–16 instead of 1.1–1.8, with a 3–4× shear-rate spread at fixed φ and a 2.37× pure-fluid calibration defect, because it drove a *body force* through a stationary channel and compared *nominal* rates. §3.7 replaces all four defects: moving-wall Couette (the c = 0 case is checked against the exact solution τ = ρνγ̇ at 2 %), η_r as a ratio of measured viscosities, closures evaluated at φ_lattice, and both stress estimators carried through every point. What survives the rework is *not* a pass: the dilute point agrees with Einstein, the concentrated point does not agree with Krieger-Dougherty, and §3.7 reports that as a FAIL of the pre-declared criterion rather than as an apparatus artefact.
2. **Einstein/KD are volume-fraction closures**; they carry no shear-rate dependence. The μ_r produced by the chain is therefore γ̇-independent by construction (φ = 0 gives a flat curve, checked in c4) — that is a property of the closure, not a numerical artefact to be suppressed.
3. **Resolution matters for the LBM criterion.** At H = 8 the L2 error of the power-law profile is 2.33 %, i.e. *above* the 2 % node criterion; H = 12 gives 1.06 % and the production H = 16 gives 0.57–0.75 %. The test therefore runs H = 12 (seconds) and the artifact H = 16; the coarse case is reported here rather than hidden behind a loose tolerance.
4. **Lattice rescaling.** For φ ≥ 0.05 the physical Krieger-Dougherty consistency pushes the relaxation time to O(10) in lattice units, so the solver is given (λK, λa). The velocity field is exactly invariant under that rescaling (momentum balance is linear in both), so the verification target is unchanged; λ is recorded per case in §3.5 and `ν_wall,physical` is the physical value.
5. **The KD and Thomas curves diverge at high concentration** — their agreement is a mid-concentration statement, which is why c2 is restricted to 0.10 ≤ φ ≤ 0.30. Checked off-line: at φ = 0.40 the two differ by 8.8 % and at φ = 0.50 by 6.7 %, so no claim is made there.
6. **Applicability.** Below the gel/percolation threshold the slurry is a viscous (power-law) fluid and this closure applies. Above it the material carries a yield stress; that regime is the separate Herschel-Bulkley surface (`tensorlbm.frazil_hb_rheology`), not this node.
7. **Not modelled:** floc break-up kinetics (n is a function of φ only, not of the shear history), temperature dependence of μ_water, and the ice-crystal aspect ratio (spheres in the Einstein/KD sense). These are declared gaps, not fitted knobs.

Reading the Couette rows: the apparatus is calibrated to 0.016 % against an exact solution and the dilute viscosity is within a few per cent of Einstein, so the **method** is within 3 %; the concentrated point is not, and §3.7 says so. So: every quantity that has a closed-form reference is **within 3 %**; the only comparison above 3 % is the model against the *independent* Thomas correlation (+7.492 %), which is a different functional form fitted to a different data set — it is inside the predeclared 10 % criterion but is *not* claimed to be a 3 % agreement.
